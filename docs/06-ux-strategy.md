# 06 — UX Strategy

## Core Problem

The central UX problem is not simply lack of information.

It is **uncertainty**.

Patients may be uncertain about:

- Pain
- Cost
- Doctors
- Treatment options
- Treatment duration
- What will happen during the first appointment

---

# Strategic Principle

> Reduce uncertainty before asking the patient to book.

---

# Five UX Pillars

## 1. Comfort

Treat dental anxiety as a first-class experience problem.

## 2. Transparency

Give patients useful information about costs and treatment variables.

## 3. Trust

Show expertise through doctors, philosophy and evidence.

## 4. Education

Explain procedures and decisions in language patients can understand.

## 5. Friction Reduction

Connect treatment discovery, estimation and booking.

---

# Key UX Decisions

## Decision 1 — Global Anxiety Mode

The patient can identify themselves as nervous without navigating to a separate page.

This preference is reflected across the experience.

## Decision 2 — Interactive Treatment Finder

The patient can filter services instead of scanning a long static list.

## Decision 3 — First Visit Timeline

The experience explicitly explains what happens during the appointment.

## Decision 4 — Structured Case Studies

Evidence is presented as a journey rather than only an image comparison.

## Decision 5 — Cost Estimator

The interface provides an estimated range while clearly communicating that final pricing depends on clinical factors.

## Decision 6 — Contextual Booking

Previously selected treatments and comfort preferences can be carried into booking.

```text
Understanding

Trust
   ↓
Cost Clarity
   ↓
Personalization
   ↓
Booking
```

---

# Why This Architecture?

The architecture follows the patient's questions instead of the clinic's internal organizational structure.

This shifts the website from:

> "Here are our services."

to:

> "Here is everything you need to confidently decide what to do next."
